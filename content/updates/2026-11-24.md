## DWD ICON-EU, icosahedral

The ICO in ICON stands for icosahedral: the grid of triangles the model actually computes in. For years, ICON-EU went out into the world dressed in lat/lng. No longer: ICON-EU is ready to be true 2 itself.

<mark>TLDR: If you use ICON-EU, point to `dwd-icon-eu-forecast-5-day-icosahedral` now!</mark>

Starting December 1, DWD will publish ICON-EU on its **icosahedral grid only**. We've released a new version: [DWD ICON-EU forecast, 5 day, icosahedral](https://dynamical.org/catalog/dwd-icon-eu-forecast-5-day-icosahedral/) is live and updating.

- Europe at 6.5 km resolution, five-day forecasts initialized every six hours. The archive starts 2026-10-07.
- It's the model's own grid, with no resampling in between. That skips the oversampling a lat/lng grid needs, so the dataset is about 13% smaller.
- Space is a single `cell` dimension instead of latitude and longitude. That can take some getting used to, so we've included the metadata that helps common libraries work with it, and the [quickstart notebook](https://github.com/dynamical-org/notebooks/blob/main/dwd-icon-eu-forecast-5-day-icosahedral.ipynb) walks through spatial selection and regridding.
- The existing [lat/lng ICON-EU dataset](https://dynamical.org/catalog/dwd-icon-eu-forecast-5-day/) stays available with its longer history, but **it stops updating after November 30, 2026**.

## NOAA RTMA, RTMA-RU, and URMA

Three NOAA near-surface analyses are now in the catalog. All cover the continental United States at 2.5 km resolution. These are virtual datasets, referencing NOAA's GRIB files and optimized for map access.

- [NOAA RTMA analysis, virtual](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-rtma-analysis-virtual/): hourly analyses, with an archive starting 2017-05-02.
- [NOAA RTMA-RU analysis, virtual](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-rtma-ru-analysis-virtual/): rapid updates every 15 minutes, with an archive starting 2017-12-13.
- [NOAA URMA analysis, virtual](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-urma-analysis-virtual/): hourly analyses incorporating later-arriving observations, with an archive starting 2017-05-02.

RTMA combines observations with a model background. URMA trades speed for a more complete historical analysis and serves as the National Weather Service's analysis of record for forecast verification. Check the catalog pages for examples and the variable lists.
