HRRR wakes up every hour, on the hour, and runs a fresh forecast over the whole of CONUS at 3 km. Four times a day it keeps going out to 48 hours; the other twenty times it stops at 18. Until now we only shipped the four.

## noaa-hrrr-forecast-18-hour-virtual is now available

[noaa-hrrr-forecast-18-hour-virtual](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-hrrr-forecast-18-hour-virtual/) is the hourly companion to [the 48 hour virtual dataset](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-hrrr-forecast-48-hour-virtual/) we released in July. That one carries only the 00, 06, 12, and 18 UTC runs — the ones long enough to reach 48 hours. This one carries **every** initialization, stepping hourly out to 18 hours. If you want the freshest HRRR forecast at any given moment, this is the one to read.

![noaa-hrrr-forecast-18-hour-virtual](https://dynamical.org/assets/catalog-thumbnails/noaa-hrrr-forecast-18-hour-virtual.jpg)

Like its 48 hour sibling, it's virtual, low-latency, and optimized for spatial (map) access: all 176 variables, 50 model levels and the pressure levels, stored on HRRR's native Lambert Conformal Conic grid with spatial indexing along `x` and `y`. The archive runs from 2018-07-13 to the present.

Reading it needs `dynamical-catalog>=0.7.0` (or `zarr>=3.2`, `icechunk>=2.0`, and `gribberish>=1.5`). The notebook shows how to use the embedded spatial reference to pull out an area of interest.
