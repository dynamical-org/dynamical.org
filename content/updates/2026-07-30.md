## NASA IMERG Early and Late are live

IMERG combines observations from a constellation of satellites into a global precipitation analysis. We now have two live-updating IMERG datasets in the catalog:

- [NASA IMERG Early](https://dynamical.org/catalog/nasa-imerg-analysis-early/) is available about 4 hours after observation time.
- [NASA IMERG Late](https://dynamical.org/catalog/nasa-imerg-analysis-late/) incorporates more observations and both forward and backward propagation, with a latency of roughly 12 to 18 hours.

Both are global, 0.1° (~10 km), half-hourly, and extend back to 1998. They include precipitation rate and a precipitation quality index.

![NASA IMERG Early](/assets/catalog-thumbnails/nasa-imerg-analysis-early.jpg)

We are releasing the Early and Late runs of IMERG V07. When NASA releases V08, we plan to add the Final run as well.

## STAC + Icechunk

Every catalog page now includes two ways to open a dataset: with [`dynamical-catalog`](https://github.com/dynamical-org/dynamical-catalog), or directly from our [STAC catalog](https://stac.dynamical.org/catalog.json) with Icechunk.

The examples use `pystac` because it keeps the code concise, but any HTTP client can read the same STAC metadata. The important part is that the STAC is the source of truth for the current Icechunk asset:

```python
import icechunk
import pystac
import xarray as xr

catalog = pystac.Catalog.from_file("https://stac.dynamical.org/catalog.json")
collection = catalog.get_child("noaa-gfs-forecast")
asset = collection.assets["icechunk-https"]

repo = icechunk.Repository.open(icechunk.http_storage(asset.href))
session = repo.readonly_session("main")
ds = xr.open_zarr(session.store, chunks=None)
```

One request: don't copy the asset URL out of STAC and hard-code it into your application. We may move or version stores in the future. Resolve the current location from STAC when you open the dataset, or let `dynamical-catalog` do that for you.

This is a good time to remind everyone that if you are still accessing data products via `data.dynamical.org`, it is deprecated and will be **discontinued at the end of August**.

## When is a forecast late?

"What then is time? If no one asks me, I know; if I wish to explain it, I do not."

You all made Saint Augustine proud with your questions, and prompted a deeper dive into when a forecast should be considered "delayed" (among other explorations).

The short?

- switched to delay threshold of p95 + max(p95 - p50, 15 minutes) which does a decent job across all sources whether they have a really tight distribution (HRRR) or a wide one (ECMWF AIFS ENS)
- analyzed whether lead group delays using spreadf can detect the full run being delayed, or if they often recover

The longer version, including what we learned from replaying roughly a year of arrivals, is in our living [lab notes](https://dynamical.org/research/when-the-forecast-is-ready/).

The findings resulted in improvements to our [pipeline status](https://dynamical.org/status/pipeline/).

## Upstream dissemination advisories

We now ingest dissemination advisories from NOAA and ECMWF, maps them to the products we monitor, and shows them alongside observed delays. We backfilled the advisory archive to early 2022 and now track new and resolved notices in real time.

These are useful as two separate signals: our arrival monitoring tells us what is happening in the files, while an agency advisory can tell us why. Advisories are available on the [pipeline status page](https://dynamical.org/status/pipeline/), in the public [status feed](https://assets.dynamical.org/wxopticon/feed.json), and as a webhook event.

## New variables

Thank you for your requests! We added a few requested variables to existing datasets:

- [GFS analysis](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-gfs-analysis/) and [forecast](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-gfs-forecast/): 80 m wind components, temperature, and pressure
- [GEFS analysis](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-gefs-analysis/) and [35-day forecast](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-gefs-forecast-35-day/): 80 m wind components, temperature, and pressure
- [HRRR analysis](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-hrrr-analysis/) and [materialized 48-hour forecast](https://dynamical.org/catalog/noaa-hrrr-forecast-48-hour/): visible-beam downward solar flux, or direct/beam surface irradiance

The exact variable names and coverage are in each catalog entry.
